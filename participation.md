  <h1>Participation and FAQ</h1>
  <p class="lede">A Rally is a few days of shared work. You will get the most from it if you like unfinished plots, other people's questions, and putting your name next to theirs when the help was real.</p>

<article class="prose">
  <h2>Before you apply</h2>
  <p>Read the <a href="{{ '/about/' | relative_url }}">about page</a> for how the meetings run, and the <a href="{{ '/code-of-conduct/' | relative_url }}">code of conduct</a> for how people are expected to treat one another. Applying means you are willing to follow both. The first Rally's form, deadline, and decision date are still <strong class="tbd">TBD</strong> on <a href="{{ '/events/first-rally/' | relative_url }}">its page</a>. There is no way to apply ahead of that form.</p>

  <h2>Questions</h2>

  <h3>Who is a Rally for?</h3>
  <p>Anyone who wants to work, in person, on the Roman data that the event is allowed to use. That includes observers, theorists, statisticians, and people whose main craft is software. Students are welcome. So are people who have never touched a Roman file and are honest about that.</p>

  <h3>Do I have to be a member of the RSC?</h3>
  <p>Membership in the Roman Science Collaboration is voluntary, and it is not required to do science with Roman data. It is also not required to apply to a Rally, unless a particular event page adds that rule. The first Rally does not. Joining the collaboration is explained on the <a href="https://outerspace.stsci.edu/spaces/RSCPUB/pages/286851875/Roman+Science+Collaboration+RSC+Public+Page+Home">RSC public page</a>, including sponsorship for students.</p>

  <h3>Do I need a finished project?</h3>
  <p>No. Bring a question, a worry about the data, or a skill you are willing to lend. Some people arrive with a plan. Others join a plan on the first morning. Both are ordinary.</p>

  <h3>Will there be talks?</h3>
  <p>A short orientation, and brief check-ins so groups can find each other. The rest of the time is for working. If you are preparing a long slide deck, you are preparing for a different kind of meeting.</p>

  <h3>How are people chosen?</h3>
  <p>When a meeting has more applicants than seats, the organizers try to admit a mix of skills, scientific interests, and career stages, with attention to early-career astronomers. The written criteria for the first Rally are <strong class="tbd">TBD</strong>. They will be posted on the event page before the form opens, so applicants can see the rules they are walking into.</p>

  <h3>Can I attend remotely?</h3>
  <p>In-person work is the point of a Rally, because the useful part is overhearing the next table. Whether a given meeting offers any remote role is <strong class="tbd">TBD</strong> and will be written on that event's page. A satellite gathering at another institution is a better fit for many people than a video window into a room they are not in.</p>

  <h3>Is there funding?</h3>
  <p>Travel support, lodging, and help with dependent care are <strong class="tbd">TBD</strong>. Any registration fee is <strong class="tbd">TBD</strong>. The event page will say who is eligible and how to ask. Please wait for that note before you assume the answer in either direction.</p>

  <h3>What should I bring?</h3>
  <p>A laptop, a way to take notes you are willing to share, and any code you are free to show the other participants. Slides are optional. Data and software you are not allowed to share should stay at home. Notes about the room itself, including power and meals, belong on the event page once a host is named.</p>

  <h3>What data will we be allowed to use?</h3>
  <p>Rules for commissioning and early products are set by the mission. The event page will name the products that are in bounds. For the first Rally, that note is <strong class="tbd">TBD</strong>.</p>

  <h3>Can my institution host a Rally or a satellite?</h3>
  <p>Yes. That is how the series is meant to grow. A satellite is a smaller gathering held at the same time as a main Rally, for people who cannot make the trip. Write via the <a href="{{ '/contact/' | relative_url }}">contact page</a> with a city, a rough season, and the room you have in mind. The contact address is <strong class="tbd">TBD</strong>, so hosting requests have to wait for it. What the organizers can offer a host (publicity, a shared application, nothing at all) is also <strong class="tbd">TBD</strong>.</p>

  <h3>Who do I tell if something is wrong in the room?</h3>
  <p>The <a href="{{ '/code-of-conduct/' | relative_url }}">code of conduct</a> explains how to report. The confidential address is <strong class="tbd">TBD</strong>. Please do not post a conduct report as a public GitHub issue.</p>
