  <h1>Participation and FAQ</h1>
  <p class="lede">A Rally is a week of shared work. You will get the most from it if you like unfinished plots, learning something new, other people's questions, and putting your name next to theirs when the help was real.</p>
</header>

<article class="prose">
  <h2>Before you apply</h2>
  <p>Read the <a href="{{ '/about/' | relative_url }}">about page</a> for how the meetings run, and the <a href="{{ '/code-of-conduct/' | relative_url }}">code of conduct</a> for how people are expected to treat one another. Applying means you are willing to follow both. The first Rally's form, deadline, and decision date are still <strong class="tbd">TBD</strong> on <a href="{{ '/events/first-rally/' | relative_url }}">its page</a>. There is no way to apply ahead of that form.</p>

  <h2>Questions</h2>

  <h3>Who is a Rally for?</h3>
  <p>Anyone who wants to work, in person or on Zoom, on the Roman commissioning data. That includes observers, theorists, statisticians, and people whose main craft is software. Students are welcome as are those who have no prior experience with Roman.</p>

  <h3>Do I have to be a member of the RSC?</h3>
  <p>Membership in the Roman Science Collaboration (RSC) is voluntary; but strongly encouraged for Rally participants. Joining the RSC is explained on the <a href="https://outerspace.stsci.edu/spaces/RSCPUB/pages/286851875/Roman+Science+Collaboration+RSC+Public+Page+Home">RSC public page</a>, including sponsorship for graduate students.</p>

  <h3>Do I need a finished project?</h3>
  <p>No. Bring a question, a worry or curiosity about the data, or a skill you are willing to lend. Some people arrive with a plan. Others join a plan on the first morning. Both are ordinary.</p>

  <h3>Will there be talks?</h3>
  <p>A short orientation, and brief check-ins so groups can find each other. We may have a few tutorials by experts that you can join if the area interests you. The rest of the time is for working.</p>

  <h3>How are people chosen?</h3>
  <p>When a meeting has more applicants than seats, the organizers try to admit a mix of skills, scientific interests, and career stages, with attention to early-career astronomers. The written criteria for the first Rally are <strong class="tbd">TBD</strong>. They will be posted on the event page before the form opens, so applicants can see the rules they are walking into.</p>

  <h3>Can I attend remotely?</h3>
  <p>In-person work is valuable but not critical. We will offer remote participation via Zoom and Slack. But if you have >3 people at your local institution interested in participating, then we recommend joining as a Rally Room.</p>

  <h3>Is there funding?</h3>
  <p>There is no registration fee; but, sadly, also no funding support for the Roman Rally.</p>

  <h3>What should I bring?</h3>
  <p>A laptop, a way to take notes you are willing to share, and any code you are free to show the other participants. Data and software you are not allowed to share should stay at home.</p>

  <h3>What data will we be allowed to use?</h3>
  <p>Rules for commissioning and early products are set by the mission. All public data products are in bounds.</p>

  <h3>Can my institution host a Rally Room?</h3>
  <p>Yes! A Rally Room is a smaller gathering held at the same time and on approximately the same schedule as the main Rally, for people who cannot travel as far. Write via the <a href="{{ '/contact/' | relative_url }}">contact page</a> with a city, local organizer, and the institution and room you have in mind. Organizers must be able to arrange a small meeting space and do some local advertising.</p>
