  <h1>Code of conduct</h1>
  <p class="lede">Roman Rally meetings are for scientific work. That work depends on people being able to ask questions, share unfinished ideas, and stay in the room. This code is the standard for every Rally.</p>
</header>

<article class="prose">
  <p>Astronomy meetings commonly publish a code along these lines: how we treat each other, what is unacceptable, how to ask for help, and what the organizers may do. The wording here is ours. It is adapted from that meeting custom, including the expectation, shared with the <a href="https://aas.org/policies/ethics">American Astronomical Society Code of Ethics</a>, that participation is free from harassment. It does not replace the AAS code. Policies of the Roman Science Collaboration apply as well. A direct link to the RSC policy document the organizers want cited is <strong class="tbd">TBD</strong>. The collaboration's public home is on <a href="https://outerspace.stsci.edu/spaces/RSCPUB/pages/286851875/Roman+Science+Collaboration+RSC+Public+Page+Home">Outerspace at STScI</a>.</p>

  <h2>Where this applies</h2>
  <p>This code covers the meeting days, meals and social gatherings organized around a Rally, satellite events that use the Roman Rally name, and the chats, repositories, and shared documents created for a Rally. It applies to every participant, including organizers, volunteers, and guests. It applies whether the conversation is in the room or online.</p>

  <h2>What we expect</h2>
  <ul>
    <li>Treat people as colleagues. That includes students, staff, and anyone who is new to Roman data.</li>
    <li>Welcome questions, including basic ones. Critique the plot, the method, and the claim.</li>
    <li>Share credit. If someone contributed a substantial piece of a project, offer them a co-author place on the papers that come out of it. If you accepted that kind of help, make the offer.</li>
    <li>Ask before you photograph or record someone, and before you quote a private conversation in public.</li>
    <li>Stop when you are asked to stop.</li>
    <li>Follow the rules of the host building, and leave the room usable for the next group.</li>
  </ul>

  <h2>What we do not accept</h2>
  <p>Harassment, intimidation, and sustained disruption are outside this meeting. That includes, without trying to make a complete list:</p>
  <ul>
    <li>Comments, images, or jokes that single people out by gender, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, disability, physical appearance, body size, race, ethnicity, age, religion, national origin, or career status.</li>
    <li>Unwelcome sexual attention, stalking, or deliberate intimidation.</li>
    <li>Interrupting or talking over someone as a way to shut them out of the work, or otherwise disrupting a group after being asked to stop.</li>
    <li>Publishing a person's private information, or the contents of a private report, without their permission.</li>
    <li>Retaliation against someone who raises a concern or helps someone else raise one.</li>
  </ul>
  <p>Scientific disagreement is part of the meeting. Humiliating a person is not a form of scientific disagreement.</p>

  <h2>Open work</h2>
  <p>Projects at a Rally are developed in the open among the people there. Bring ideas, code, and data you are allowed to share with the room. The fuller account of credit and co-authorship is on the <a href="{{ '/about/' | relative_url }}">about page</a>. When a project is carried forward as Roman Science Collaboration work, RSC publication policy applies. The link organizers want on this page is <strong class="tbd">TBD</strong>.</p>

  <h2>How to report</h2>
  <p>If you can, tell a member of the Local Organizing Committee or the Scientific Organizing Committee. You may ask them to keep your name out of the next conversation, and you may bring someone with you. For the first Rally, those names are <strong class="tbd">TBD</strong> on the <a href="{{ '/events/first-rally/' | relative_url }}">event page</a>.</p>
  <p>A confidential address for reports, including reports you would rather not make in person, is <strong class="tbd">TBD</strong> on the <a href="{{ '/contact/' | relative_url }}">contact page</a>. Please do not file a conduct report as a public GitHub issue. The repository is fine for typos in this website. It is the wrong place for a complaint about a person.</p>
  <p>If you are not comfortable contacting the Rally organizers, you can reach the Roman Science Collaboration through its public page and ask to be directed to a spokesperson. That page is a public collaboration space, so treat it as a way to find a person, not as a private inbox.</p>
  <p>You can also report conduct that violates the AAS Code of Ethics through the Society's own process. A Rally report and an AAS report are separate. Making one does not block the other.</p>
  <p>Write down what you remember when you can: roughly when, roughly where, and what you are asking the organizers to do. You do not have to assemble a legal case to be heard.</p>

  <h2>What organizers may do</h2>
  <p>Organizers will take reports seriously. They may warn someone, ask them to leave a session, end their participation in the meeting and its channels, or refuse a future application. They may involve the host institution or a relevant professional body when the situation calls for it.</p>
  <p>Whether a registration fee exists, and whether any fee is returned if someone is required to leave, is <strong class="tbd">TBD</strong>. There is no promise of a particular outcome. There is a promise of a response, and a promise that retaliation for a good-faith report is itself a violation of this code.</p>

  <h2>Who carries this</h2>
  <p>The Scientific and Local Organizing Committees for each Rally are responsible for upholding this code at that meeting. Their names, for the first Rally, are <strong class="tbd">TBD</strong>. RSC policies apply alongside this page wherever the collaboration's rules cover the same people and the same work.</p>
