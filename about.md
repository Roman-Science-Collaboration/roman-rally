  <h1>About and philosophy</h1>
  <p class="lede">Roman Rally is a series of working meetings. The Roman Science Collaboration organizes them so people from many institutions can spend a few concentrated days on data from the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope.</p>
</header>

<article class="prose">
  <h2>An unconference, on purpose</h2>
  <p>The agenda is built by the people in the room. A Rally opens with proposals, not with a program of invited talks: a plot someone wants to try, a cross-match, a calibration that looks wrong, a question that needs a different statistic. Groups form around those proposals. The schedule is a shared document, and it is expected to move.</p>
  <p>Check-ins stay short. A few minutes to say what you are doing is enough for the rest of the room to find you. The hours in between are for the work.</p>

  <h2>Projects that organize themselves</h2>
  <p>You can arrive with a plan, or you can join one. Projects split, merge, and sometimes stop. A good Rally ends with a collaboration that did not exist at the start: a draft, a repository, a list of coauthors, a clearer question. It does not need a proceedings volume.</p>
  <p>Anyone who does a substantial part of a project can expect an invitation onto the papers that come out of it. Anyone who accepts that kind of help is expected to offer the invitation. Notes and code written at the meeting are shared with the other participants. If a dataset or a library cannot be shared with the room, leave it at home.</p>
  <p>Some projects will go on to be published as work of the Roman Science Collaboration, and some will be ordinary papers among the people who wrote them. Which path a project takes, and the RSC publication rules that apply, are <strong class="tbd">TBD</strong> until the organizers point this page at the policy they want cited. The collaboration's public home is on <a href="https://outerspace.stsci.edu/spaces/RSCPUB/pages/286851875/Roman+Science+Collaboration+RSC+Public+Page+Home">Outerspace at STScI</a>.</p>

  <h2>Room for people who are early, or new here</h2>
  <p>Rallies are meant to be small enough that a student can ask a basic question and be heard. When more people apply than a room can hold, the organizers aim for a wide mix of skills, science topics, and career stages, with real space for early-career participants. You do not need to be the Roman person in your department. You do need to be ready to work in the open and to credit the people next to you.</p>
  <p>Membership in the Roman Science Collaboration is voluntary. The RSC explains on its own site how to join, including a way for members to sponsor students and others who do not yet meet the usual publication criteria. Joining the RSC and applying to a Rally are separate steps. An event page will say if a particular meeting adds a requirement. The first Rally does not.</p>
  <p>The <a href="{{ '/code-of-conduct/' | relative_url }}">code of conduct</a> is part of how the room stays usable. Harassment and score-settling are outside the meeting. So is the quiet habit of talking over the same three people.</p>

  <h2>Who makes a Rally happen</h2>
  <p>Each meeting has a Scientific Organizing Committee and a Local Organizing Committee. The scientific committee looks after the purpose of the meeting, the application, and the balance of the participant list. The local committee looks after the room, the skeleton of the schedule, and the practical questions: building access, meals, a quiet space, and how to report a problem in person.</p>
  <p>Names for the first Rally are <strong class="tbd">TBD</strong>. They will be listed on <a href="{{ '/events/first-rally/' | relative_url }}">that event's page</a>.</p>

  <h2>What the series is for</h2>
  <p>The meetings are for commissioning and first-look data, the stretch when the observatory is being characterized and the first public products are new enough that a shared room helps. Roman launched on 30 August 2026. NASA has said it expects to release the first images by early 2027. Later Rallies can follow later releases. This website will say, on each event page, which products are in scope. For the first Rally, that list is <strong class="tbd">TBD</strong>.</p>
  <p>A Rally is a community meeting for doing science with data the mission has already made available. Mission reviews, proposal rankings, and official data announcements belong to NASA, the Science Centers, and the panels they convene. Mission status lives on <a href="https://science.nasa.gov/blogs/roman/">NASA's Roman blog</a>.</p>

  <h2>Satellites and later hosts</h2>
  <p>A later Rally can be hosted by another institution, and a satellite can run in parallel for people who cannot make the trip. How a group asks to host one, and what support the organizers can offer, is <strong class="tbd">TBD</strong>. The <a href="{{ '/contact/' | relative_url }}">contact page</a> will carry the address. Until then, the invitation stands: the series is meant to move.</p>
