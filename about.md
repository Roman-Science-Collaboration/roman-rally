  <h1>About and philosophy</h1>
  <p class="lede">Roman Rally is a series of working meetings. The Roman Science Collaboration organizes them so people from many institutions can spend a few concentrated days on data from the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope.</p>
</header>

<article class="prose">
  <h2>An unconference, on purpose</h2>
  <p>The agenda is built by the people in the room (virtual or physical). A Rally opens with proposals, not with a program of invited talks: a plot someone wants to try, a cross-match, a calibration that looks wrong, a question that needs a different statistic. Groups form around those proposals. The schedule is a shared document, and it is expected to move.</p>
  <p>Check-ins stay short. A few minutes to say what you are doing is enough for the rest of the room to find you. The hours in between are for the work.</p>

  <h2>Projects that organize themselves</h2>
  <p>You can arrive with a plan, or you can join one. Projects split, merge, and sometimes stop. A good Rally ends with a collaboration that did not exist at the start: a draft, a repository, a list of coauthors, a clearer question. </p>

  <p>Anyone who does a substantial part of a project can expect an invitation onto the papers that come out of it. Anyone who accepts that kind of help is expected to offer the invitation. Notes and code written at the meeting are shared with the other participants. If a dataset or a library cannot be shared with the room, leave it at home.</p>
  <p>Some projects will go on to be published as work of the Roman Science Collaboration, and some will be ordinary papers among the people who wrote them. Which path a project takes, and the RSC publication rules that apply, are <strong class="tbd">TBD</strong>. 

  <h2>Room for people who are early, or new here</h2>
  <p>Rallies are meant to be small enough that a student can ask a basic question and be heard. When more people apply than a room can hold, the organizers aim for a wide mix of skills, science topics, and career stages, with real space for early-career participants. You do not need to be the Roman person in your department. You do need to be ready to work in the open and to credit the people next to you.</p>
  <p>Membership in the Roman Science Collaboration is voluntary but strongly encouraged. The RSC explains on its <a href="https://outerspace.stsci.edu/spaces/RSCPUB/pages/286851875/Roman+Science+Collaboration+RSC+Public+Page+Home">own site</a> how to join, including a way for members to sponsor students. Joining the RSC and applying to a Rally are separate steps.</p>

  <p>The <a href="{{ '/code-of-conduct/' | relative_url }}">code of conduct</a> is part of how the Rally stays workable. Please adhere.</p>

  <h2>Who makes a Rally happen</h2>
  <p>Each meeting has an Organizing Committee that looks after the purpose of the meeting, the application, and the balance of the participant list, the room, the skeleton of the schedule, building access and how to report a problem in person.</p>
